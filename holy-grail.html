Research Notes

The Holy Grail of Investing

Yicheng Yang · April 18, 2026 · 14 min read

Abstract. This note evaluates a family of trend-following strategies on the leveraged ETF TQQQ, using QQQ as the signal-generating instrument. All strategies are tested via causal forward walk-forward starting in 1999 (with synthetic pre-2010 TQQQ) and validated by combinatorial purged cross-validation (CPCV) following López de Prado (2018). Three findings: (i) the widely-cited “47% annualized” figure for static EMA crossover is in-position CAGR, not calendar CAGR — the calendar value is 26.45%; (ii) under causally-clean forward walk-forward, the pure TQQQ pyramid with half-base MA anchoring achieves Calmar 0.310 (single-path point estimate), modestly above QQQ/TQQQ rotation at 0.282 and substantially above buy-and-hold; (iii) CPCV reveals that the apparent ranking among the four strategies is fragile — median CPCV Calmar across 45 paths for the rotation is 0.079 with interquartile range [−0.026, 0.259], implying that the single-path forward result is one realization of a noisy process, not a stable edge.

1. Setup and Cost Model

Daily price data for QQQ and TQQQ were sourced from Yahoo Finance and cross-validated against Alpha Vantage's daily endpoint over fourteen consecutive trading days; matched closing prices agreed to four decimal places. All returns are computed on adjusted closes with auto-adjustment for splits and dividends. The TQQQ series begins on its 2010-02-11 inception; for the pre-2010 window I use a synthetic series constructed as

rTQQQ,tsynth = 3 · rQQQ,t − (eannual + ft + sannual) / 252,

where eannual = 0.84% is the ProShares expense ratio, ft is a piecewise-constant financing cost based on the federal funds rate plus a 40 bps spread applied to the 2× borrowed portion, and sannual = 0.30% accounts for daily rebalancing slippage. Validating the synthesis against the live 2010-2026 series, the synthetic recovers the 16-year compound multiple to within 5.4% (262.3× vs 248.0× synthetic; implied annual drift of −0.35%/yr).

All backtests apply a 2.5 bps commission and a 5 bps slippage on each side of every position change, with T+1 settlement (signals at close on day t are filled at close on day t+1). Strategies are evaluated by CAGR, maximum drawdown (MDD), annualized Sharpe ratio, Sortino ratio, and the Calmar ratio (CAGR/|MDD|).

2. The 47% Headline

The most widely circulated retail strategy in this category is a static EMA(5)/EMA(200) crossover applied directly to TQQQ, with full position on golden cross and full cash on death cross. Promotional materials report a CAGR of 47.08%. Reconstructing the strategy with the cost model above yields:

53 Trades over 2010-2026 (matches the cited count)
$420K Final value from $10K (within 1.4% of the cited result)
26.45% Calendar-time CAGR over the full 16-year window

The discrepancy is mechanical. A 47.08% compound rate over 16 years would produce a terminal multiple of 1.470816 ≈ 480×, or roughly $4.8M from $10K. The actual terminal multiple is 42.0×. Solving for the period over which 47.08% compounds to 42.0× yields 9.72 years — matching the in-position duration of the strategy if cash periods are excluded. The cited figure is therefore an in-position CAGR, computed only over the days the strategy holds TQQQ. Under the same convention, a strategy that holds TQQQ for one day per year and returns 50% on that day would report 50% “annualized.”

Pitfall 1. When evaluating any reported CAGR, verify that the denominator is calendar time. In-position CAGR — the geometric mean computed only over days the strategy holds a position — can be inflated arbitrarily by long cash periods and is not directly comparable to buy-and-hold returns.

3. Methodology: Why Forward Walk-Forward Matters

A common backtest pattern in quantitative-finance writing is to select strategy parameters on a recent in-sample window (e.g., 2010-2026) and then evaluate the chosen strategy on an earlier “out-of-sample” window (e.g., 1999-2010). This pattern is anti-causal: the parameter selection uses information unavailable at the historical decision date. A 1999 investor cannot consult 2010-2026 returns when calibrating a strategy.

The causally-clean alternative is forward walk-forward: the test always sits chronologically after its training window. I run the following protocol throughout this note:

  • Training window. 5 calendar years of QQQ data ending at time t.
  • Embargo. 21 trading days (one month) between training and test, per López de Prado (2018) to prevent label leakage from autocorrelated returns.
  • Test window. 2 calendar years immediately after the embargo.
  • Selection. Within each training window, the (fast, slow) EMA pair maximizing Calmar is chosen from a 25-element grid (fast ∈ {3, 5, 8, 10, 13}, slow ∈ {50, 100, 150, 200, 250}). For pyramid variants, the level schedule and base position are fixed ex ante.
  • Roll. The window advances by the test length and re-trains.

The first viable test window starts in 2004 (after 5y training from 1999-03 plus warm-up for the 200-day EMA). Buy-and-hold benchmarks are reported over the same window for comparability.

For statistical robustness, I additionally apply combinatorial purged cross-validation (CPCV): the data is partitioned into 10 chronological folds, all C(10,2) = 45 combinations are used as test sets, and the remaining 8 folds form the training set with embargo applied at fold boundaries. Each path produces an independent estimate of CAGR, Sharpe, and Calmar. The distribution of these 45 estimates is the basis for the inference reported in Section 6.

4. Strategy Taxonomy

Four strategies are evaluated, all driven by EMA crossover signals computed on QQQ and traded with TQQQ exposure. The choice of QQQ rather than TQQQ as the signal source is deliberate: TQQQ's daily-leverage construction introduces noise into trend signals that the underlying index does not have.

  • TQQQ/Cash. Bull signal: 100% TQQQ. Bear signal: 100% cash.
  • QQQ/TQQQ rotation. Bull signal: 100% TQQQ. Bear signal: 100% QQQ — eliminating the opportunity cost of cash during slow uptrends below the moving average.
  • Pure TQQQ pyramid (peak-anchored, T4). Bull signal: 100% TQQQ. Bear signal: cash reserve, deployed to TQQQ in 25/50/75/100% tranches as the index falls 10/20/30/40% from its rolling all-time high.
  • Pure TQQQ pyramid (half-base MA, T6). Bear regime starts at 50% TQQQ + 50% cash, with the cash reserve deployed in the same tranches as T4 but anchored to MA200 instead of the rolling peak.

Negative-result variants — SQQQ inverse leverage, ML signal aggregators, VIX-thresholded regime filters — were also evaluated. SQQQ in particular is structurally incompatible with long-horizon holding: 3× daily inverse exposure on a long-term upward-trending index converges to zero by construction (the synthetic 1999-2026 series falls below 10−5 of starting value). These results are omitted from the discussion below.

Two observations are notable. First, contrary to results obtained under anti-causal selection schemes, the pyramid strategies do not fail catastrophically in the 1999-2010 sub-period when their parameters are chosen using only data available at each historical decision point. The peak-anchored pyramid (T4) loses substantially during the dot-com crash but survives because its bull/bear EMA selector keeps it in cash for most of the 2000-2002 decline; the cash-deployment tranches engage only during the 2002 acceleration to the lows. Second, TQQQ buy-and-hold is the only strategy whose terminal value is essentially flat over 27 years — the dot-com collapse pushes the synthetic series to less than 0.02% of starting value, from which 16 years of bull-market compounding recovers only to roughly the original starting capital.

6. Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation

Single-path forward walk-forward delivers point estimates but no inference about variability. With 11 test windows in the 2004-2026 horizon, a single CAGR estimate could plausibly differ from the underlying expected return by a substantial margin. CPCV addresses this by generating 45 independent backtest paths from the same data, each using a different 8-fold/2-fold split with embargo.

The CPCV distributions reveal what the single forward walk-forward estimate hides:

Strategy Median CAGR CAGR IQR Median Calmar P(CAGR > 15%)
QQQ/TQQQ rotation 5.24% [−1.81%, 13.71%] 0.079 24.4%
TQQQ/cash 3.97% [−1.60%, 12.32%] 0.068 17.8%
Pyramid T4 (peak) 2.99% [−9.30%, 14.43%] 0.058 24.4%
Pyramid T6 (half-base MA) 4.83% [−3.37%, 14.81%] 0.085 24.4%

The CPCV medians are substantially below the single-path forward walk-forward estimates, and the interquartile ranges include zero for every strategy. The four strategies' median Calmars cluster between 0.06 and 0.09 — close enough that any ranking among them is statistically indefensible. The probability of achieving CAGR above 15% is roughly 1-in-4 to 1-in-5 across all variants.

Two interpretations of the gap between single-path forward and CPCV estimates are consistent with the data. First, the 2004-2026 forward path may be a favorable sample of all possible 22-year evolutions of these strategies; the secular bull market post-2009 is the dominant contributor to the high single-path CAGR estimates. Second, CPCV assigns equal weight to test paths that include the dot-com unwind, the 2008 crisis, and the 2022 selloff in different combinations — some of which are more punishing than the actual chronological sequence. Both effects are real; the truth probably lies between the single-path and the CPCV-median estimates.

Pitfall 2. A single backtest path is one realization, not an estimate of expected performance. CPCV (or block bootstrap on returns) is required to produce a defensible distribution. Single-path point estimates that look impressive should be checked against their CPCV percentiles before they are interpreted as edges.

8. Statistical Considerations

Forward walk-forward parameter selection eliminates look-ahead bias at the parameter level. Two layers of post-hoc choice nonetheless remain:

  • Window length. The 5y/2y choice was selected after evaluating combinations from (3y, 1y) to (7y, 3y); the median CAGR across the nine tested combinations is 21.2% with IQR [14.7%, 24.4%], suggesting that the headline 20.6% point estimate for the rotation is approximately representative rather than cherry-picked.
  • Strategy family. Four strategies are reported; an equal number of negative-result variants (SQQQ rotation, ML aggregators, VIX-filtered) are not. Under a Bonferroni correction with eight evaluated families, the t-statistics for the reported winners should be divided by approximately √8 ≈ 2.83 to recover unbiased significance levels.

Combining the CPCV distributions with the window-selection and family-selection corrections, the most defensible reading of the data is: the four leveraged-ETF strategies have expected CAGR somewhere between 5% and 15%, expected Sharpe somewhere between 0.4 and 0.7, and expected Calmar somewhere between 0.08 and 0.20. The single-path forward walk-forward estimates that put pyramid T6 at Calmar 0.31 and rotation at 0.28 are upper-bound realizations rather than central tendencies. None of the four strategies dominates the others by a margin that survives the variance.

Pitfall 3. Forward walk-forward eliminates look-ahead within a strategy but does not eliminate selection bias from variant choice or window choice. Each layer of post-hoc selection inflates the apparent significance of the reported winner. The minimum standard for reproducibility is to report the full distribution of variants and windows tested, not only the best.

9. Conclusions

Three conclusions follow.

First, the most-cited 47% “annualized” figure for static EMA(5)/EMA(200) on TQQQ is in-position CAGR, not calendar CAGR. The calendar value computed from the same trades and the same data is 26.45%. This distinction matters because in-position CAGR is not directly comparable to buy-and-hold returns and can be inflated arbitrarily by long cash periods.

Second, under causally-clean forward walk-forward starting in 1999, the four tested strategies all generate positive CAGR — including the pure TQQQ pyramid, which under anti-causal evaluation appears to fail catastrophically in the dot-com era. The pyramid T6 variant achieves the highest single-path CAGR (25.66%) and the highest single-path Calmar (0.310). The QQQ/TQQQ rotation achieves the lowest single-path drawdown (−73%) among leveraged variants. The differences among the four strategies are real on the single forward path but small.

Third, combinatorial purged cross-validation reveals that these single-path differences are not statistically distinguishable. Across 45 CPCV paths, the four strategies have median Calmars between 0.058 and 0.085, with interquartile ranges that all include zero. The probability of achieving CAGR above 15% is approximately 1-in-4 across all four variants. The single-path forward walk-forward estimates sit in the right tail of these distributions, indicating that the apparent edge over QQQ buy-and-hold is one realization of a noisy process rather than a stable risk-adjusted improvement.

The realistic menu of leveraged-ETF strategies is therefore narrower than the single-path numbers suggest. A trend-following strategy on TQQQ, however constructed, delivers somewhere between a 1-in-4 and 1-in-5 chance of meaningfully outperforming unleveraged QQQ buy-and-hold on risk-adjusted terms over a 22-year horizon. The other three-quarters of the time, the strategy delivers performance comparable to or worse than QQQ buy-and-hold while subjecting the portfolio to drawdowns of 70% or more. There is no parameterization within these families that resolves the underlying volatility-decay asymmetry of 3× daily leverage; this is a property of the instrument, not the strategy.
